# Kew Trademark Policy

The Kew **source code** is available under the [Functional Source License](LICENSE).
The Kew **name and logo are not** — they are trademarks of Pedro Henrique Gomes
Rodrigues Marques (the "Steward"). This split is intentional and standard for open and
source-available projects: the license lets you use and build on the code, while the
trademark lets users trust that "Kew" means the genuine project.

This policy explains how you may refer to Kew. It is deliberately permissive for
honest, non-confusing use.

## You don't need permission to

- Use the name "Kew" to **refer to the project** truthfully — "built with Kew",
  "compatible with Kew", "a Kew plugin".
- State that your product **works with** or **integrates with** Kew.
- Run Kew, including in production and self-hosted for your own client, as the license
  allows.
- Write tutorials, reviews, talks, or articles about Kew.
- Say, factually, that a fork is "based on Kew" — as long as you don't name the fork
  itself "Kew".

## You need written permission to

- Use "Kew" (or a confusingly similar name) as the **name of your own product,
  service, company, or app** — including a hosted or modified version.
- Use the **Kew logo** other than to link to or reference the project.
- Use the name or logo in a way that **implies endorsement, affiliation, or that your
  offering is the official Kew**.
- Register "Kew" (or a confusingly similar mark), or a domain that does, in any
  jurisdiction or app store.

## Forks

You may fork the code — the license allows it. You **must give the fork a different
name** and remove the Kew logo, so users are not misled into thinking it is the
official project. You may, and should, state factually that it is "based on Kew".

## Nominative use

When you refer to Kew, use the name only as much as needed, don't style it to look like
your own brand, and don't suggest the Steward sponsors you.

## Questions / permission

To request permission or ask about a specific use, contact the Steward via
[kew.sh](https://kew.sh). When in doubt, ask — the goal is only to prevent user
confusion, not to limit honest use.
